## Overview

The team reviewed functional design presentations from D and B regarding remote control user needs and technical constraints, while C introduced material and cost considerations for the circuit board design. Discussions focused on product strategies, including target demographics, material integration, and feature feasibility. The group debated button layouts, accessibility features, and power options. Key decisions were made regarding the product's universal nature, durability, and specific button placements, while several features like speech recognition and LCD screens were tentatively agreed to be excluded or require further cost analysis before final inclusion.

## Main topics

### Product Strategy and Target Demographics

The team discussed product design strategies, including material integration, target demographics, and the feasibility of features like LCD screens, speech recognition, and universal remote capabilities. They ultimately decided to pursue a universal remote with changeable covers while discarding speech recognition. The project targets young people under forty who are willing to pay for fashionable and functional designs. The group also tentatively agreed that the remote control must feature the corporate colors black and yellow and that the design should be distinct from competitors to avoid confusion. Decisions were made to prioritize durability and clear symbols, with the Teletext button retained but made less prominent as it is not a primary feature for the target demographic.

### Feature Feasibility and Design Constraints

The team discussed the feasibility and design implications of adding an LCD screen and speech recognition to a remote control, debating button placement options and agreeing to research the costs and space requirements for these features. It was tentatively agreed that speech recognition and LCD screens are possible but expensive, and the LCD screen will be removed due to high power consumption and fabrication costs. The group also tentatively agreed that the remote control must include buttons for all basic functions to ensure compatibility with various TV brands. Additionally, the team tentatively agreed to prioritize a design that includes a rechargeable option pending cost analysis, and they must research the costs and space requirements for both an LCD screen and speech recognition before making a final choice.

### Remote Control Layout and Button Placement

The team discussed remote control design requirements, including button layout, universal functionality across TV brands, and accessibility features like visual feedback for the deaf. They tentatively agreed that the channel and volume buttons should be illuminated on their own to be easily visible and that the power button should be located in a central position, though later tentatively agreed it must be at the top to prevent accidental presses. The volume control buttons must be arranged with the higher value at the top and the lower value at the bottom, with the mute button positioned at the bottom separate from the volume selection area. The group also tentatively agreed that important buttons must be placed on top and made larger, and that the most frequently used functions must be represented prominently on the remote.
