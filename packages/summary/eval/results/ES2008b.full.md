## Overview

The team reviewed market research indicating a strong user preference for aesthetically pleasing remotes and simple interfaces. They discussed design trade-offs between feature-rich and minimal designs while troubleshooting presentation software. Key decisions were made to exclude teletext and voice recognition due to cost and obsolescence, focusing exclusively on television functionality. The group settled on prioritizing simplicity, fashion, and ergonomics, aiming for a conventional layout that avoids confusion while incorporating novel ideas to stand out. Specific constraints include a production cost limit of twelve and a half Euros and a target demographic of individuals aged fifteen to thirty-five.

## Main topics

### Market Research and Design Trade-offs

The team began by reviewing market research showing that seventy-five percent of users find current remotes ugly and eighty percent are willing to pay more for a fancier look. This led to a discussion on the trade-offs between creating a feature-rich interface versus a simple one. While presenting technical and working design concepts, the team also encountered and began troubleshooting issues with their presentation software. The conversation focused on how to balance technological capabilities with economic constraints, specifically weighing the cost of advanced features like voice recognition against the need for design improvement to ensure the product does not look like an eyesore on a coffee table.

### Functional Requirements and Exclusions

The group reviewed the working design schematic and addressed new requirements regarding the exclusion of teletext functionality, noting it has been superseded by the internet. They debated the target demographic and feature set within a strict production cost limit of twelve and a half Euros. Decisions were made to exclude voice recognition because it would exceed the budget and is inversely related to age preferences. The team also ruled out a Ceefax-style remote concept due to management decisions. The focus shifted strictly to television-only functionality to ensure a quick release, excluding video or other formats, while ensuring the infra-red signal remains reliable and clear to prevent user frustration.

### Design Priorities and Demographics

Discussions centered on settling on simplicity and fashion as the core project goals to win over consumers who desire non-ugly remotes. The team debated the feasibility of voice recognition again before defining the target demographic as individuals aged fifteen to thirty-five, though some notes suggest a broader range under sixty-five. The design must be conventional and familiar to avoid confusion while allowing for novel ideas to stand out. Ergonomics were highlighted as a priority to prevent hand injuries, requiring large accessible buttons for essential functions and a mechanism to hide complicated buttons. The final product must incorporate the corporate image of yellow and black colors with the slogan 'fashion in electronics' and be sold as a separate product priced at twenty-five Euros.
