## Overview

The team reviewed remote control designs, focusing on user demographics and new project requirements. Key decisions included limiting the device to television-only use to reduce complexity and costs, and targeting a younger market under forty. The group decided to implement an LCD screen to appeal to this demographic while considering a backup plan if costs become prohibitive. Discussions also covered features like speech recognition, docking stations, and button layouts, with several items remaining tentative or proposed pending further investigation into costs and functionality.

## Main topics

### Target Demographics and Device Scope

The team discussed the need to shift focus toward a younger market demographic, specifically customers under forty, while maintaining the corporate image. It was decided that the new product must target this younger group rather than the current older base. Furthermore, the group resolved to limit the remote control exclusively to television use, avoiding other appliances like video or stereo systems. This decision aims to reduce project complexity, shorten the time to market, and lower overall costs. The consensus was that the device should not be used for other functions, ensuring a streamlined product scope.

### LCD Screen Implementation and Cost Management

The team agreed to implement an LCD screen on the new remote control to appeal to the younger target market, replacing traditional rubber buttons. A decision was made to proceed with this feature, though a backup plan was established in case costs become prohibitive. If the LCD screen proves too expensive, the team will revert to using rubber buttons. The group also tentatively agreed to include a docking station for recharging and a low battery indicator on the screen. Action items were assigned to investigate specific production costs and post relevant email information to the project folder.

### Button Layout, Features, and Sub-menus

Discussions centered on the implementation of sub-menus for teletext, screen, audio, and channel settings on a buttonless remote control. It was decided that teletext functionality should be accessible via a separate menu on the LCD screen rather than a dedicated button, and less important functions should be placed in sub-menus. The remote must include a mute button, battery indicator, and controls for volume and channel. While speech recognition was proposed as an optional feature dependent on budget, the specific power-on mechanism remains unresolved, with the decision to postpone the discussion regarding a physical button versus screen tap to the next meeting.
