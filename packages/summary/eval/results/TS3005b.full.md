## Overview

The team reviewed remote control designs, focusing on user demographics and new project requirements. Key decisions included limiting the device to television-only use to reduce complexity and costs, and targeting a younger market under forty. The group decided to implement an LCD screen instead of rubber buttons to appeal to this demographic, though a backup plan exists if costs become prohibitive. Discussions covered features like a docking station, mute button, and sub-menus for settings. While teletext was deprioritized, speech recognition remains a tentative option dependent on the remaining budget. Several action items were assigned to investigate costs and finalize button layouts.

## Main topics

### Target Demographics and Device Scope

The team reviewed user demographics and clarified that the new remote control must target a younger market, specifically customers under forty, while maintaining the corporate image. A significant decision was made to limit the device exclusively to television use, avoiding other appliances like video or stereo systems. This restriction was intended to reduce project complexity, shorten the time to market, and lower overall costs. The group also decided that the design must incorporate the company's corporate color and slogan to ensure a recognizable brand identity. These constraints define the core scope of the project moving forward.

### Display Technology and Button Design

Discussions centered on replacing traditional rubber buttons with an LCD screen to better appeal to the younger target demographic. The team decided to implement an LCD screen on the remote control, with a tentative agreement to revert to rubber buttons only if the screen proves too expensive. The design will feature no physical buttons, relying entirely on screen interaction for functions like power, volume, and channel selection. A mute button and a battery indicator were also decided upon as essential features. Additionally, the remote will automatically shut down when the TV enters standby mode, and a low battery warning will appear before the battery is empty.

### Feature Implementation and Accessibility

The team debated specific features, deciding that teletext functionality should be deprioritized or excluded due to its decline in popularity and the rise of the internet. Instead, teletext will be accessible via a separate menu on the LCD screen. Less important functions such as screen settings, audio settings, and channel settings were proposed to be placed in sub-menus rather than having dedicated buttons. The remote will include a docking station for recharging and support multiple languages including French, Dutch, English, Chinese, and Swahili. Speech recognition was tentatively agreed upon only if sufficient budget remains after other features are funded.
