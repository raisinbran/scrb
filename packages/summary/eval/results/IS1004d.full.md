## Overview

The team reviewed the final design prototype of a banana-themed remote control, evaluating its features, functionality, and aesthetic choices. They scored the prototype against usability, functionality, and market appeal criteria, debating how to aggregate the results before moving to cost estimation. The group finalized production cost estimates by making several decisions to reduce costs and scope. Key decisions included removing LCD screens and speech recognition, adopting a banana theme, and selecting specific button configurations. The team also addressed potential health and safety concerns regarding the design and agreed on a target selling price of 25 Euros for the European market.

## Main topics

### Design Prototype Review and Scoring

The team began by reviewing the final design prototype of the banana-themed remote control, discussing its features, functionality, and aesthetic choices. They proceeded to score the prototype across multiple criteria including usability, functionality, and market appeal. A significant portion of the discussion focused on how to aggregate these scores for the final evaluation. The group tentatively agreed not to sum individual scores but instead rely on a general feeling or average. They also settled on a logical criteria scale ranging from one to seven to ensure sufficient granularity in the evaluation process. This discussion ended with a transition to a cost estimation exercise.

### Cost Estimation and Scope Reduction

The team reviewed and finalized the production cost estimates for the banana remote control project, making several decisions to reduce costs and scope. They tentatively agreed that the total cost must fit within a target of twelve point five Euro and the target selling price is set at 25 Euros. To meet these financial constraints, the group decided to exclude solar cells, switch the case material from rubber to plastic, and replace rubber buttons with plastic ones. They also decided to remove the sample speaker component from the bill of materials and use a regular chip instead of an advanced one. These changes were made to ensure the project stays under budget while maintaining core functionality.

### Feature Specifications and Final Decisions

The group accepted that the remote control design will not include LCD screens or speech recognition technology. They decided that the remote and its base station will feature a banana-themed look and feel. Navigation will use scroll wheels instead of traditional buttons, and the turbo button will serve as the on/off switch. The design includes scroll wheels for volume and channel control, a turbo button for skipping channels, and a teletext button. The base station will feature a button to call the remote and leaves that act as antennas. Power will consist of rechargeable batteries with charging via the base station's power line input, and the remote will have an autonomy of eight to ten hours. The project scope is limited to TV remotes, excluding universal remotes for other devices.
