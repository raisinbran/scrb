## Overview

The team reviewed previous action items and presented initial research on consumer needs and technical design for a new remote control. Key discussions focused on narrowing the project scope to television-only functionality, establishing specific design requirements regarding form factor, branding, and usability, and addressing concerns about lost remotes and repetitive strain injuries. The group also assigned specific tasks for component analysis, user evaluation, and trend research to prepare for the next session.

## Main topics

### Project Scope and Functional Limitations

The team refined the project strategy by deciding to limit the remote control to television-only functionality, explicitly excluding universal remote capabilities. This decision was driven by user research indicating that most users do not utilize the full range of buttons found on complex universal remotes. Additionally, the group tentatively agreed to remove speech recognition features due to high costs and reliability issues, and ruled out teletext functionality. The scope was further defined by decisions to exclude rarely used buttons like programming functions, which should be hidden away to maintain a simple, user-friendly interface. This focus aims to reduce project complexity and cost while addressing the specific needs of the target audience.

### Design Requirements and Aesthetics

Significant attention was given to the physical and aesthetic requirements of the new device. The team decided that the remote must feature a modern appearance to change the current perception of remotes as ugly, incorporating the company's corporate colors of grey and yellow with grey or black buttons. A slogan stating 'we put the fashion in electronics' must be pasted on the bottom of the device. The design must be operable with one hand, primarily catering to right-handed users, and feature a minimal number of buttons to ensure simplicity. The group also decided to include a scrolling function, potentially implemented as a wheel, and tentatively agreed that the device must be powered by a battery rather than a cable.

### Ergonomics, Features, and Next Steps

The discussion addressed ergonomic concerns and specific feature additions to enhance usability. The design must prevent repetitive strain injury, and the team tentatively agreed to explore a two-piece modular system where a smaller device handles basic functions like channel and volume control while the main unit fits comfortably in the hand. To address user frustration with losing remotes, the project tentatively agreed to include a tracking device or locator beacon, potentially integrated into a holder or charger. Specific standard buttons were identified, including numbers one through nine, volume and channel controls, power, mute, and a menu button. Action items were assigned to Manuel for component analysis, Ryan for user evaluation, and Speaker C for investigating mobile phone design trends.
