## Overview

The team reviewed previous action items and presented initial research on consumer needs and technical design for a new remote control. Key discussions focused on narrowing the project scope to television-only functionality, rejecting universal features and speech recognition due to cost and reliability. The group established specific design requirements regarding form factor, button layout, and corporate branding. Several tentative agreements were reached on user-centered design principles, while specific tasks were assigned to team members for the next session to further explore concepts and gather data.

## Main topics

### Project Scope and Feature Selection

The team refined the remote control strategy by deciding to limit the project to TV-only functionality, explicitly excluding universal remote capabilities. Speech recognition was ruled out due to high costs and reliability concerns. The group tentatively agreed to remove rarely used buttons like teletext and programming functions to simplify the interface. Decisions were made to exclude cable power sources and teletext features, focusing instead on a battery-powered device with a minimal number of buttons to ensure a simple, user-friendly experience that prevents repetitive strain injury.

### Design Requirements and Aesthetics

Discussions centered on establishing a modern, fun, and sturdy appearance that addresses user frustration with current ugly remotes. The design must incorporate corporate colors, specifically grey and yellow, with black as a potential alternative, and include the slogan 'we put the fashion in electronics' on the bottom. The remote must be operable with one hand and primarily cater to right-handed users. A rounded design was tentatively agreed upon to fit comfortably in the hand, aiming to set a new trend in remote control fashion while maintaining a user-interface oriented approach.

### Form Factor and Action Items

The team explored various design concepts, including a potential two-piece modular system where a smaller device handles basic functions like channel and volume control. A tracking device or locator beacon was tentatively agreed upon to address the issue of lost remotes. Specific tasks were assigned, with Manuel tasked to identify components and explore the two-piece concept, while Ryan will evaluate how well the proposed concepts work with users. The group also agreed to distribute questionnaires and summaries to facilitate individual work on components and user interface concepts before the next meeting.
