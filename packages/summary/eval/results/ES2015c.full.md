## Overview

The meeting focused on finalizing the design specifications for a new remote control project. Speaker B presented research on components, leading to tentative agreements on kinetic energy sources and rubber casings. The team debated interface designs, ultimately deciding on a command interface while considering fruit and vegetable themes. Discussions covered material choices, button functions, and power systems, with several decisions made regarding the chip type and display exclusion. Action items were assigned to specific members for further research and design work before the next session.

## Main topics

### Power Source and Internal Components

The group extensively discussed the energy requirements for the remote control. Speaker B presented research leading to a tentative agreement on using kinetic energy as the primary power source to eliminate batteries. Decisions were made to exclude LCD displays and backup batteries due to budget constraints and the desire for simplicity. The team also settled on using a regular chip for the circuit board. However, questions remain regarding whether users will shake the device enough to generate sufficient power, and there is a need for further research into the practicality of the kinetic technology.

### Exterior Design and Materials

Discussions centered on the physical construction of the remote, specifically the casing and button aesthetics. The team tentatively agreed to use a hard plastic exterior coated with rubber for protection and a trendy look. While initial thoughts included literal fruit and vegetable images, the group decided to tone this down in favor of abstract shapes to maintain a sophisticated appearance. Decisions were made to use black buttons that light up when touched, possibly utilizing translucent rubber. The team also committed to generating five different casing ideas to explore various design possibilities.

### User Interface and Functional Layout

The team finalized the user interaction model and button configuration for the device. A command interface was decided upon for its simplicity and user-friendliness, replacing more complex graphical options. Functionally, the remote will feature push buttons rather than scroll buttons, limited to basic functions like power, channel selection, and volume. Poppy was assigned to handle the look-and-feel design, while she and Tara will collaborate on the user interface using modelling clay. Speaker C is tasked with explaining the interface concept, and the group must decide if all buttons should remain the same color for a simplistic look.
